Your data stays yours.

Streamora plays movies, shows and anime from your own TorBox account. It has no Streamora accounts and no database of its own. This page says what it stores, where, and who else receives what.

The short version

  • Your library lives in one file in your own Google Drive, and a copy lives on your device.
  • Streamora can't see any other file in your Drive.
  • Your TorBox key is shared with TorBox and the built-in source services, because they need it to find sources for your account.
  • No analytics, no ads, no tracking cookies. Your data is never sold.
  • You can delete it all from Settings → Privacy.

Google sign-in and Drive

When you sign in with Google, Streamora asks for your name, email address and profile picture, to show which account you're using. It also asks for access to only the files Streamora creates in your Drive (the drive.file permission). It makes one folder called "Streamora" with one data file inside.

That file holds your profiles, watchlists, watch progress and history, diary, settings, your TorBox API key and, if you connected it, your Trakt sign-in. This is how your devices stay in step. The app in your browser reads and writes the file directly.

Streamora's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Your Google data is used only to save and load your Streamora data. It is never sold, shared, used for ads or used to train AI models.

On your device

A copy of your data is kept in your browser's localStorage, so the app opens quickly and works offline. Your TorBox key is encrypted there. Sign-in tokens for Google and Trakt are kept in the browser's IndexedDB.

The welcome film and landing page store a few small notes in localStorage: whether you've seen the film, when you last visited (so the film can return after a long absence), and whether you turned page sounds on. These stay on your device.

Streamora's relays

A few small server functions on Cloudflare Pages forward requests that a browser can't make itself. Our code doesn't save or log what passes through them. Cloudflare hosts the site, so it handles your IP address and request details as any web host does.

  • /api/tbPasses requests to TorBox with your API key. When asking for a video link, it also passes your IP address, so TorBox can pick a nearby server.
  • /api/googleCompletes Google sign-in, which needs the app's client secret.
  • /api/traktCompletes Trakt sign-in, for the same reason.
  • /api/imdbLooks up titles the main catalog lacks.
  • /api/addonFetches results from add-ons that browsers can't reach directly. The add-on sees the request come from the relay.

Other services

Your browser contacts these services directly unless noted. Here is what each one receives.

  • TorBoxtorbox.app, *.tb-cdn.st and other TorBox hostsYour API key, the items and links the app manages in your account, and the video requests when you play something.
  • Torrentio, Comet, MediaFusiontorrentio.strem.fun, comet.elfhosted.com, mediafusionfortheweebs.midnightignite.meThe title you open and your TorBox key, to find sources for your account. These are independent services, each with its own privacy practices.
  • Meteormeteorfortheweebs.midnightignite.meThe title you open. It doesn't receive your key.
  • Add-ons you addSettings → ConnectThe title and episode you ask for, sent to that add-on's address.
  • Cinemetav3-cinemeta.strem.ioThe titles you browse and the words you search for.
  • Kitsuanime-kitsu.strem.fun, kitsu.ioThe same, for anime, plus lookups that match an anime to its other ids.
  • AniSkipapi.aniskip.comFor anime, the MyAnimeList id and episode number, to find intro and outro times.
  • OpenSubtitlesopensubtitles-v3.strem.ioThe title and episode you are watching, to find subtitles.
  • Metahubimages.metahub.spaceRequests for posters, backdrops and logos. The image addresses show which titles you see.
  • Wikipediaen.wikipedia.orgCast names, to fetch their photos.
  • YouTubeyoutube-nocookie.comThe trailer's video id, when you open a trailer.
  • Googleaccounts.google.com, googleapis.comSign-in, and your Streamora file in Drive.
  • Traktapi.trakt.tv, only if you connect itWhat you watch and the history you import.
  • GitHubapi.github.com, github.comThe iOS app checks about once a day for a newer release, with no personal data added. The download link opens github.com.

Any service you connect to also sees your IP address and browser details, as any website does. The landing page's film, pictures, sounds and fonts come from Streamora's own site, not from a third party.

Deleting your data

Go to Settings → Privacy → Delete everything from Drive. This removes the file from your Drive, clears the copy on that device and revokes Streamora's access to your Google account. Other devices keep their local copy until you sign out there or clear the site's data.

You can also remove Streamora's access at myaccount.google.com/connections. Your TorBox account and any history in Trakt are managed with those services.
